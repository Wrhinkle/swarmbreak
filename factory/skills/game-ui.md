# Game UI: when laying out or changing a player-facing game screen

- Treat the stage as the viewport: reserve space for the HUD and controls, respect device safe-area insets, and keep all play content inside it. Use an intentional inner scroller for long content rather than scrolling the page. Check short viewports as well as narrow ones.
- Establish hierarchy: show the current place and immediate objective first, the game state next, and secondary detail on demand. Keep the same HUD positions and meanings across screens; avoid covering the encounter or important controls with decorative panels.
- Give cards and option plates a consistent anatomy: title, concise effect or consequence, cost if any, and a clear action. Selected, disabled, focused, and available states must be distinguishable without color alone; use actual buttons with accessible labels and visible keyboard focus.
- Choose a small type scale with readable body text and restrained display headings. Use responsive sizes such as `clamp()` and allow long names and descriptions to wrap without clipping or breaking in the middle of ordinary words. Keep contrast high against art with a backing surface or overlay.
- Make tap targets comfortably sized (aim for at least 44 CSS pixels) and spaced apart. Place frequently used actions within reach; never shrink labels just to fit more options. Preserve logical focus order and test with keyboard as well as touch.
- Acknowledge input immediately, then show the resulting state: for example, highlight a selected routine and announce whether an order was queued. Transitions should connect states without delaying play or hiding information; honor reduced-motion preferences and keep the outcome legible without animation.
- Build and inspect the actual game at 390px and 1280px wide, including a short mobile height. Visit title, map, an option-heavy room, and fight where relevant; try long names, focus, disabled controls, and changes of state. Check for clipped text, tiny targets, overlapping HUD, and document scrolling during play. Capture screenshots and inspect them; automated tests alone do not establish visual quality.
