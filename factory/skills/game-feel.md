# Game feel: when presenting ATB, combat timing, or combat feedback

- Make the state readable before adding spectacle: distinguish crew and enemies, identify each fighter, and show health, shields, status, and the next action or progress toward it where the underlying rules support that information. Label gauges and pair color with text or shape.
- For ATB, show progress against a visible full threshold and make readiness distinct from charging. Telegraph actor, intended action and target before the impact when that information exists; never suggest certainty about a target or timing the simulation has not determined.
- Layer feedback in a clear sequence: anticipation, action, impact, then a brief readable result. A hit can use a short pose or flash plus a damage number and health change; healing and shields need visibly different cues. Keep effects anchored to the correct actor or target, and prevent simultaneous numbers from obscuring one another.
- Tune the duration and intensity with actual fights at each supported speed. Preserve time to understand significant events, including defeat, queued orders, and cooldowns. Avoid constant shake, flashing, or motion that obscures health or targets; provide reduced-motion equivalents that retain the information.
- Presentation must follow the deterministic combat state and events, not alter tick timing, random draws, targeting, or outcomes. Do not add a telegraph by inventing new combat rules. If a change touches combat logic, run the required balance evidence as well as deterministic tests.
- Play and inspect representative fights at 390px and 1280px, at 1× and faster speeds, with pause/resume and reduced motion. Check several fighters, overlapping actions, a boss, death, and narrow-screen text. Confirm the same seed and inputs still produce the same fight, and that the player can tell who acted, whom it affected, and what changed.
